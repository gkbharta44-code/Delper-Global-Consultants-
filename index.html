Delper Global Consultants: cross-border trade platform

The platform onboards trading parties through one API, holds buyer funds in escrow, releases them after independent inspection, and sources orders through competitive bidding. The model assumes a 14-day settlement cycle, giving 26 turns a year from Year 2 (Year 1 is 17.6 turns). Goods move through a Delper-managed bonded warehouse, and logistics providers selected by bidding deliver them to the buyer's door. Escrow, delivery and other costs are charged to the customer outside the revenue split. The earlier FOB description, and the claim that no duty-avoidance structure is needed, are not supported by the source, which describes duty-deferred bonded warehousing. Trade terms are open (see conflicts).

Revenue comes from a software take rate of 24.2% on consumer and high-margin goods (15.184% on premium commodities), a liquidity spread of 6.0% a year net on 14 to 90 day facilities secured by bonded cargo, and, from Year 6, sovereign BOOT concessions. The model applies 24.2% to all turnover and does not model BOOT revenue separately.

A CSR plan would fund village infrastructure from 2% of pretax profit.

Delper Retail: buyer-first verified sourcing

Delper Retail is a buyer-first platform for e-commerce. Buyers post enquiries, pre-vetted sellers bid in real time, an independent third-party quality control (3PQC) team inspects goods before dispatch, and buyer money sits in escrow until delivery is confirmed. Buyers pay no separate fee. Sellers pay for bidding and inspection at actual cost, a 2% commission on completed sales, and a lead generation charge deducted from sale proceeds.

  • Scope: the marketplace is India only for five years, using ONDC for buyer reach and Axis Bank for escrow. Both are in discussion, not signed. The source also says Delper Retail pilots in India and Malaysia; this is listed under conflicts.
  • Partners: SGS as target lead 3PQC partner; EY as proposed independent assurer; PwC as optional operating partner at growth stage. None is engaged.
  • Seller terms: free static catalogue; USD 100 annual onboarding fee to trade; RTB and inspection prepaid at actual cost and non-refundable.
  • Key open issue: sellers prepay costs and wait up to 15 days after delivery to be paid. The pilot is meant to test whether sellers accept this.
  • Pilot: 30–50 vetted factories, 15–25 DTC brands and 300–500 real orders over 6–9 months, on a budget of about $2.5–4M of the seed. The Series A gate is a go or no-go on cost per order, seller-paid charges and brand reorders.
  • Customer onboarding: Delper Retail is a separate profit center with its own P&L. Customers register by QR code, are tagged to the distributor that brought them, and give consent at signup. Data-protection law applies per market (DPDP Act 2023 in India; PDPA 2010 in Malaysia).

Transactions

Two separate $4.0M raises are proposed, $8.0M in total.

Transaction A: Singapore SPV raise (Delper Global Consultants)

  • Raise: $4.0M into the Singapore SPV.
  • Valuation: ₹120.8 Cr (2.5x the stated run rate of ₹48.32 Cr), about $12.6M at ₹96/$. This is the IP value of Radiant Synage's contribution. The roughly $0.6M gap to the $12.0M pre-money is hedged against INR devaluation and is disregarded.
  • Stake: 25% ($4.0M ÷ $16.0M post-money), implying a $12.0M pre-money.
  • Repayment stream: CapEx repayment equals 24.15% of revenue. The investor is repaid from its 25% share, which is 6.04% of revenue. The other 75% (18.11% of revenue) has no stated recipient in the source.
  • Proposed investor: not yet selected.
  • Dilution: a 10% option pool created before the round would reduce the stake below 25% (to 22.5% in the source's exit table).

Transaction B: Delper Retail seed (proposed)

  • Raise: $4.0M seed of Series Seed Preferred equity, or a SAFE converting into it.
  • Valuation: $12M pre-money, $16M post-money; investors own 25%. This is the base case within a planning range of $8M to $16M pre-money. The 25% excludes a 10% option pool created before the round.
  • Tranches: $1.0M at close (set-up, hiring and legal work); $1.5M when the Axis Bank escrow terms and the QC partner agreement are signed; $1.5M when the platform is live, the first sellers are certified and real orders are flowing.
  • Later rounds: Series A of about $20M at $50M pre-money and $70M post-money after pilot proof (28.6% sold); growth round of $50M at $200M pre-money and $250M post-money (20% sold). The seed stake falls to about 14.3% after both, before option pools.
Separate raises: Transaction A and Transaction B are two distinct $4.0M raises, totalling $8.0M. Each has its own vehicle, valuation and stake.

Market

  • Global Consultants: the source's addressable pool is $9.15T. Base world GDP is $176.44T in Year 11 ($126.30T in Year 1); the source does not label it a TAM. Year 11 GMV of $123.51T is about 1350% of the addressable pool. GMV counts the same money at each step of a chain, so the source divides it by chain depth: at a depth of 2, end-customer spend is $61.76T, about 35% of world GDP. Chain depth is an unverified input.
  • Retail: ONDC reported about 200 million potential buyers, roughly 500,000 sellers and about 1,000 cities by June 2026, and about 9 million transactions a month. The 7.5M-order Year 5 target is under 4% of potential buyers and about 7% of ONDC's annual transactions, which are mostly mobility and food.

Aegis

Aegis is funded in Year 5 from the net profit of Delper Global Consultants and Delper Retail. Retail's illustrative Year 5 net profit is $204.38M. No source states Global Consultants' Year 5 net profit. The Aegis summary gives total launch capex of $1.90B; whether this is the required initial investment, and each entity's share, are to be confirmed.

  • Gross upfront revenue: $7.25B across three tiers of 1M units each.
  • Program: Delper Guard industrial firewall with a managed service (Aegis-Factory as a Service). The initial hardware run is 3,000,000 units. Pricing is $6,500 upfront per production line plus $8,371.50 a month.
  • Launch economics: net upfront margin of $5.35B (73.8% of gross) after $531.5M silicon NRE and $1.37B component cost, against $1.90B total launch capex.
  • Recurring revenue: $102.5B a year at full scale.
  • Compliance pool: $7.85M confirmed; only $1.5M is itemised, leaving $6.35M unexplained.
  • Staffing: a 33,000-position model needs about 159,390 engineers, costing about $36B a year. At ₹96/$, staffing costs about $1.09M per position a year ($36B ÷ 33,000), against a $100K annual subscription.
  • Year 1 cash: the $5.3B figure assumes all units sell in Year 1, despite a 12-month rollout.

Conflicts between the two source profiles

  • Structure: Global Consultants uses a Singapore SPV and a cross-border scope. Retail is India only. Transaction A goes into the Singapore SPV; the legal vehicle for Transaction B is not stated. Open: decide the Transaction B vehicle.
  • Revenue path: Global Consultants shows Year 1 GMV of $39.0M; Retail's Year 1 is a $0.1M pilot. Year 2 is $204.8M GMV under one plan and $24.0M purchases under the other. Open: confirm which Year 2 basis is used in investor materials.
  • Take rate: both show about 24.2%, one as a share of GMV and one as a share of the order price. This needs checking to see whether the same figure is reused. The source gives a derivation (a 5.1% share of friction cost removed), but applies 15.184% to premium commodities while the model uses 24.2% on all turnover. If commodity flows earn 15.184%, revenue would be about 37% lower. Open: management to confirm the commodity share and the derivation.
  • Business model (resolved): two different products. Global Consultants is a B2B trade platform with 14-day settlement; Retail is buyer-first e-commerce through ONDC. Both use escrow, inspection and bidding, but they are separate offerings.
  • Pilot economics: each Retail order is USD 300 and earns $72.67 gross profit. The $4.0M seed is fixed spend, not a per-order cost. Spread across the 400 pilot orders it is about $10,000 per order, which is only an allocation. Fixed spend of $4.0M a year needs roughly 55,000 orders a year to break even.
  • Trade terms: the earlier profile used FOB terms with no duty-avoidance structure. The source uses a bonded warehouse with a duty-deferred structure and doorstep delivery, with customs liquidated after payment. Open: confirm the Incoterms and who clears customs.
  • Retail scope: the marketplace is India only, but Delper Retail is described as piloting in India and Malaysia. Open: decide the pilot markets.
  • Retail headline: the executive summary cites 2 billion verified products and 3.5 billion protected buyers in five years. The five-year targets are 7.5M orders and 15,000 sellers, India only. Open: align the headline with the targets.
  • Retail Year 2 expenses: the source assumes about $2.9M, but the pilot cost base is $4M a year, and the source itself says to rebuild expenses bottom-up. Open: rebuild.
  • Option pool: the Retail seed terms include a 10% pool created before the round, but the 25% stake excludes it. Open: set the pre-round pool and restate the stake.
  • Global pilot claim: the source appendix cites a USD 100M a year pilot for a tequila brand, with Malaysia as the proof-of-concept node. The Global Consultants model has no pilot year. Open: reconcile.
  • Caveat wording: Retail's statement that low-rated products cannot be rejected on the basis of their rating is a draft and needs legal review in each market. Open: legal review.
